# The small CAD bench

Commands a session calls over files in its working directory, each answering in plain text and numbers, for the local model's first project (M18 in mqucifer/project-print `ROADMAP.md`, ADR 0010 there, and mqucifer/print-shop#14). A trial on 2026-11-01 showed tools reporting success when they were wrong, so **every answer gets a second check by a different method**, and the bench says when the two differ. It never touches the printer, and it holds no model call. Motion, balance, stiffness and strain come as their own issues when a project's number fails without them.

```
scripts/bench.py build                       # once: the first image, if the disk has room
scripts/bench.py build cae                   # once: the second, with FreeCAD, for fit-on, bend and load
scripts/bench.py check                       # each image and the disk against their limits
scripts/bench.py clearance a.step b.step     # any command below, over the current directory
scripts/bench.py python script.py            # your own script, in the first image's container
scripts/bench.py freecad script.py           # your own script, in the second's, with FreeCAD
```

[The rounds](rounds.md) use it as a session's tool. A session inside the image can call the same commands directly, by mounting `src/print_shop/bench` read-only (`host.py` shows how). Nothing fences the model off: these are tools it may use, and it can write its own scripts.

## What comes back

The first line is the verdict. Then come the numbers, each with how it was read, then at least one `second check:` line, and last `saved:`, naming the text and JSON files written under `bench-out/`. Every figure a report may cite is in those files, and each says how it ran: the image's id, the code's commit, and the CPU, memory and network limits as the container read them.

| Exit | Verdicts | It means |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | `OK`, `CLEAR`, `TOUCHING`, `HELD` | both methods agree, and nothing's wrong |
| 1 | `FAIL`, `INTERFERE`, `TOO CLOSE`, `TOO LOOSE`, `NO CHANGE`, `PARTIAL`, `NOT HELD`, `HELD BY A SEPARATE BODY`, `BLOCKED` | both methods agree that something's wrong |
| 2 | `DISAGREE`, `NO ANSWER`, `UNREAD` | there's no answer to rely on: read the numbers, and don't pick one. `UNREAD` is `load`'s: what would give first has no figure to be read against |
| 3 | | the command itself was wrong |

An answer with no second check behind it becomes `NO ANSWER`, whatever it first said.

**Toolsets** (mqucifer/print-shop#37). The Sponsor, 2026-11-03: "not everything is going to be a stained glass print". So a run has the core, and a request's `## Kind` adds its kind's commands. The core is `info`, `clearance`, `readback`, `rays`, `thin`, `wall`, `pause`, `params`, `render`, `layout`, `faces`, `slice`, `fit-on` and `bend`. Stained glass adds `capture`, `panes`, `light`, `thickness` and `pieces` (mqucifer/print-shop#152, its `dome` and `flatten`, mqucifer/print-shop#154, and its seams, `came` and `ring`, mqucifer/print-shop#166), an enclosure `edges` (mqucifer/print-shop#60), and furniture `load` and `fit-bed` (mqucifer/print-shop#156). `rounds/toolsets.py` holds the sets, and [the rounds](rounds.md#toolsets) say how a run uses them. From the command line, every command works as before.

**A flat layout, read in 2D** (mqucifer/print-shop#61). On `suncatcher-3`, 12 of the 35 calls that reasoned past 20,000 characters and repeated themselves stalled on which piece or neck an inset makes, and which piece is which, and the model printed bounding boxes to find out. `layout` reads a layout the model's script writes: JSON with `pieces`, each name and its outline as GeoJSON (`shapely.geometry.mapping`) or WKT, in mm (`bench/layout.py`).
- **Each piece:** one piece or not, and each part's area, a point inside it and its box, largest first, so "which is which" is a reading. Two pieces that overlap are named, with how much.
- **Its least width:** straight across, from an edge to the edge facing back within 20°: a waist, a bar, an arm, a ring, a cell side to side. Where the sides run further apart it's a corner or a tip, which an inset moves back and never cuts, so it isn't a width. Rays square to the edge are the reading, and the nearest point facing back the second: across parallel sides the two are the same, and within 20° of parallel the second reads as little as cos 20° of the first, never more. Further apart than that, and the points' spacing (at most 0.05 mm), is `DISAGREE`.
- **Sharp tips:** a corner sharper than 75°, read from the outline's own points, with where and its angle. It narrows to nothing, so it's under any `--min-width`.
- **An inset of D** (`--inset`, mitred as a layout's pockets are, or `--join round` for the true distance): its parts, its least width, and where it cuts the piece in two or takes an arm away, each stretch of the piece narrower than 2D by its narrowest and how far it runs. A negative D is an outset.
- **The picture** comes to the model, as `render`'s does: each piece in its colour, labelled on each part, its least width in red, its tips ringed, each inset's outline in black.
- `--min-width W` fails a piece narrower than W, or with a sharp tip, saying where. Without it the answer is `OK` with its readings. It reads a layout, not the part, so no need names it.
- **Back-tested** on planted pieces with arithmetic answers (`test_a_layout_says_each_piece...`), and on layouts made from scratch, up to 61 pieces with two insets in 30 s (`~/print-shop/proofs/m18-lacked/`). The reading went through three first methods before this one, each caught by the second: chords at up to 37° from square read an arm's round end at 0.8 of its width, the widest disc that fits read a wedge's rounded tip by its cap, and Delaunay edges miss the nearest point across a cell with no two sides parallel.

### A piece map: traced, checked, fixed and built

(mqucifer/print-shop#152, mqucifer/print-shop#153, M20.) A stained-glass run was given a picture and worked out its pieces itself. On the first test from a traced scene it spent about 250 calls (7 h) before its plan, writing its own Shapely code. Then, asked to merge a sliver, run a dead end on and split a neck, it ran one morphological opening over every piece: every corner was rounded, 15 to 22% of one band's glass became grey patches up to 19 mm wide, and the glass share fell from 91% to 83%. So the bench reads a picture's pieces once, the same way every time, and each fix is a command on one piece. The model chooses each fix; the bench does the geometry and the readings (decided with the Sponsor, 2026-11-17). [The piece map](pieces.md) is the format.

- **`pieces trace IMAGE --width MM [--palette NAME[=#rrggbb],...] [--dark N] [--to FILE]`** reads flat colours between dark lines (`bench/pieces_trace.py`). The panel is the picture inside its dark outer frame on all four sides: on each, the first row or column from the picture's edge that's mostly dark, and those after it that are, so a margin outside the frame, and any speck in it, isn't the panel. The lead is each pixel with every channel under `--dark` (40), closed by a square 0.5 mm across and at least 3 pixels, so a hairline gap doesn't join two pieces and a hair of colour beside a line isn't glass. A piece is a patch of the rest joined edge to edge, a patch under 40 pixels being lead. A dark speck inside one piece, joined to no line and shorter than the lead is wide, is the picture's noise: it's filled as that piece's glass and counted in one line (`specks_filled`), so a real line that ends in glass stays one flag. Its outline is its pixels' own edges, each corner kept at its pixel's corner and each staircase between two corners read as the line or curve it stands for, within a pixel of the pixels' edges. Each ring of a patch is read on its own (mqucifer/print-shop#161): a ring too short to read a corner on, a dark speck a few pixels across, is kept less its steps, and the rest are smoothed. One such ring used to leave the whole piece as its pixels' own edges, a step at every pixel. On the operator's scene, with 55 specks, four pieces were that, of 457 to 1,173 points, and the frame built from the map was 47 MB and 10,608 faces. `speckled.png`, 20 curved pieces with five specks inside two of them, traced those two at 314 and 282 points, 806 in all, and its frame was 7.0 MB and 1,686 faces; it traces 244 points now, and its frame is 3.9 MB and 856 faces (`~/print-shop/proofs/m20-pieces-2/speckled-size.txt`). Where two smoothed outlines cross, over a line a pixel or two wide, each loses what they share and half a pixel round it, so a pixel's line lies between them and both stay smooth (mqucifer/print-shop#161). Each kept its pixels' own edges before, as a piece with a speck did. `thin_line.png`, 20 curved pieces with one line a pixel wide, traced two such pieces of 262 and 230 points, 724 points in all, and its frame was 6.0 MB and 1,341 faces; it traces 263 points now, and its frame is 4.2 MB and 917 faces (`~/print-shop/proofs/m20-pieces-2/thin-line-size.txt`). A picture whose lines are all one pixel wide had no answer, its pieces overlapping, and still has none: its lead is thinner than the map's pixels can join. Its colour is the nearest of `--palette`, or the nearest colour the bench has a name for. The reply's picture comes to the model: the picture, and beside it the map with each piece numbered and each rule break marked. **Second method:** the map drawn back at the picture's scale over the patches it came from, which may differ by a pixel's width along every outline. It uses what the image already had, SciPy and Shapely: the operator's prototype read the same pixels with OpenCV and scikit-image. It reads a rectangular panel; a round one isn't traced yet.
  - **A `--dark` other than the default says what it cost** (mqucifer/print-shop#172): the pieces, the lines and the dead ends it reads (`lines_count`, `dead_ends_count`), against the default darkness on the same picture (`pieces_at_default_dark`, `lines_at_default_dark`, `dead_ends_at_default_dark`), in one line, and a note that `pieces retrace` reads one place at that darkness and keeps the rest. A line is two pieces with a line of their own between them, no further apart than a line and a quarter. On a traced scene (2026-11-18) a run lost one dark patch by tracing the whole picture at `--dark 18`, and 41 of its lines broke into dead ends: the reply that did it said nothing of them.

  With every answer: the **glass share**, the pieces' area over the panel's, against the share the map's source had, and the **lead's widest point**. `FAIL` with any flag, naming the fix for each kind. The limits are the operator's tracer's on the first traced scene, and each is an option.
  - **A second check measures the same thing, and carries its resolution** (mqucifer/print-shop#161, decided with the Sponsor, 2026-11-17). On the first eval with these commands five of the first 25 calls ended `DISAGREE`, none of them two readings of one thing. A neck read 1.022 mm by the nearest points and 1.828 mm by rays square to its edges, at the same place: on a slanted neck the rays run the slant's long way, another line. And a notch read 2.77 mm deep by its outline and 1.74 by its pixels, its end too thin for a pixel to show. After every split `check` still ended `DISAGREE`, so a real one couldn't be told from these.
  - **`DISAGREE` is two readings of the same thing on opposite sides of a limit, each beyond the other's resolution** (`limits.apart`). Closer than the coarser one's resolution they're the same reading, and what's between them is a note, even with the limit there. Further apart on one side of the limit they agree on the rule, and the gap is a note.
  - **A neck's two readings** are the same line across: the nearest points, and twice the distance from the place's middle to the nearest pixel that isn't the piece's glass, since the nearest edge to the middle of the narrowest place is one of its two ends. Those pixels are drawn round the place, 64 to the narrower of its width and the limit, so the reading is good to 3.4 of them (a pixel at each end, and half a diagonal for where the middle lies): 0.05 mm on a 1 mm neck. `slanted.json` reads 1.022 mm by both, with the rays' 1.83 mm a note. `hairs.json` is the control: two cuts two microns wide, their ends 1 mm apart, which the outline has and no pixel shows, so the pixels read no edge within 2.5 mm, and that's `DISAGREE`.
  - **A dead end's pixel reading** looks a lead and a quarter round each lead pixel for one piece's glass and nothing else. So it can't surely show a line under two pixels wide, or one with another piece, or the panel's edge, nearer than that all along: such a dead end is flagged by its outline, and a note says the pixels can't show it and why. A trace of the operator's scene at `--dark 18`, where lines go a pixel or two thin beside narrow pieces, ended `DISAGREE` this way ("isn't lone lead by the pixels"); `thin_lines.json` is that, drawn in code. A line inside a piece nearer than two pixels to other lead isn't looked for as a body of its own either, as the pixels join the two. And a dead end is matched to the pixels by where it lies: a lead pixel alone with its piece, in the dead end's own shape. It was matched by the middle of the stretch of lead it's part of, which is far off where the line all but meets a long line inside the same piece (`joined.json`): on the scene a dead end 5.4 mm long and 0.68 mm wide, with nothing within 13 mm of it, wasn't found.
  - **Lead under a nozzle's width is flagged on the map** (`thin-lead`, mqucifer/print-shop#161). On the operator's scene a frame failed `thin` at ten places, the thinnest 0.135 mm, and the run learned it only after building: the map had 17 pairs of pieces under 0.4 mm apart, the nearest 0.16 mm. Each is now a flag with its two pieces, how wide and where, and its fix: `pieces widen` first, which keeps the line, and `pieces merge` only where the two are one colour and the picture doesn't mean the line. Sixteen of the scene's 17 lay between two pieces of one colour, a sun's rays, a river's inner lines and bush outlines: lines the picture means, which a merge would take out. Lead between a piece and the panel's edge isn't read, as the frame's border stands outside it (`close.json`).

**A figure at its limit** (mqucifer/print-shop#72). On `eval-fit-3` `pause` said "0.400 mm above the pane at the pause, over 0.4 mm", and failed: the room was 0.4 by the file's figures and 0.40000000000000036 by the floats that add up the layers. `clearance` held its limits to a millionth of a millimetre, `pause` to nothing, and a need compared a figure the reply had rounded to four places. Now one rule holds every limit on the bench and every need's `at_least` and `at_most` (`bench/limits.py`):
- **A figure equal to its limit, to arithmetic's noise, passes.** Arithmetic's noise is `ARITHMETIC`, 1e-6 in the limit's unit, said once. It's a constant, not a band: no limit is loosened past it. Each comparison is `limits.under` or `limits.over`: `clearance`'s and each place's gap range, `pause --max-gap` and `--min-room`, `thin`'s nozzle and layer, `--min-body`, `light --min-open`, `thickness --max` and `--min`, `wall --min-wall`, `edges --min-radius`, `capture`'s lip and rim and share, `panes --min-area`, `layout --min-width`, `readback --min-share` and `--expect-delta`'s `--tol`, and `rays --d`'s and `--depth`'s `--tol`.
- **The figure a limit judges is the figure saved,** unrounded: `gap_mm`, `room_over_pane_mm`, `open_pct`, `least_wall_mm`, the least radii, `thickest_mm` and `thinnest_mm`, `width_mm`, `across_mm`'s ends, `floor_mm.min` and `change_mm3`. A need on it reads what the bench judged. The lines round them for print.
- **A figure said beside its limit** has as many places as it takes to tell it from the limit, so a reply never says a figure over its limit with the limit's own digits: "0.4001 mm above the pane at the pause, over 0.4 mm. Raise the pocket's floor by at least 0.0001 mm".
- **A reading's own error stays as it was,** each stated with its source, never widened, and held through the same rule (the operator's answer on mqucifer/print-shop#72): `wall --min-wall` 2 × the fine mesh's chord, 0.004 mm, as the sections are on the mesh; `edges --min-radius` 0.5% of R or 0.005 mm, OCCT's curvature on a spline; `thickness --max` and `--min` 0.004 mm, the chords; `capture`'s rim 0.01 mm and share 0.5 points, the chords on both outlines. A figure that passes only within one says so: "the 2.004 mm asked for, within the mesh's 0.004 mm". Reading these three against an exact figure, as `clearance` does, is for a later batch.
- **`thin` read a wall exactly the nozzle's width as thin:** its rays started 1e-5 mm inside the surface and didn't add that back, and the sections were opened by the nozzle's full width. A wall 0.4 mm wide at a 0.4 mm nozzle was `DISAGREE`; now it's `OK`, and 0.4001 `FAIL`.
- **Planted:** `frame.step` with a 1.6 mm pane has the room fit-3 had, 0.40000000000000036: `--max-gap 0.4 --min-room 0.4` is `OK`, where it was `FAIL`; 1.5999 is 0.4001 over and 1.6001 0.3999 under. Each other limit is run at its figure and just past it (`test_*_at_their_limit*`, `test_*_at_its_limit*`), and the before and after are in `~/print-shop/proofs/m18-gaps/`.

**Replies that say where** (mqucifer/print-shop#77). The learning loop's first class, from the Sponsor's session of 2026-11-09: tool replies that don't say enough. In 40 long calls read one at a time, a reply that left the model unsure was the main or second cause in 14, and on `pi5-case-1` the model wrote 24 measuring scripts of its own for what the bench had no command for. Each reply now says where, and says what its figures mean:
- **`clearance` leads with a file's bodies.** A file of more than one solid (or closed body, on an STL) is said first, in the verdict ("lid.step: 5 bodies; 15 pair(s): ...") and on a line naming each body's volume and box, with what to do if it's meant to be one piece. `numbers.bodies` has each. The second reading is the file's mesh, split where nothing joins it; a mesh that joins two solids where they touch is noted. On `pi5-case-1` the lid's clip legs were floating, 5 bodies, and five replies listed 15 pairs without saying so: the model found it on its own an hour later.
- **Each overlap says where it is.** Each separate piece of it, largest first, by the mesh's own intersection: its volume and the box it fills, and which named `--box` it lies inside, partly in, or "outside every named box" (`pairs.N.overlap_at`). OCCT's `common` gives its box too, in the second check (`overlap_occt_box`). The run's replies gave the volume and nothing else.
- **Two methods that both find an overlap say so.** Where OCCT's `common` and `cut` and the mesh all find an overlap and only the volumes differ, the verdict is `INTERFERE`, with "both methods find an overlap; volumes ..." and each method's figure (`overlap_by`, `volumes_differ`). `DISAGREE` stays for one method finding an overlap and another none, said in those words. A method's figure within its own tolerance of the mesh's is no disagreement, as before. On `pi5-case-1` OCCT read 4.000 mm³ and the mesh 3.274, and about 1.5 h went on a `DISAGREE` that was a volume. On the hinge turned 3e-5°, OCCT reads 1937.2 and the mesh 1933.5: `INTERFERE`; turned -3e-4° at y 0.6, OCCT reads none: `DISAGREE`. The check is as strong as it was: an overlap any method finds and every method agrees on is still a fail.
- **Many pairs shrink to what matters.** Past 3 pairs, every pair whose verdict and places' verdicts are `CLEAR`, `TOUCHING` or `NOT CAUGHT` is one counted line, with the least gap among them, and one second check; every other pair, and every place that isn't one of those, is said whole, as `pairs.N` so a need can name it. A box's own line is left out past 3 pairs, as the command gave it. The record keeps every pair. With `--box` on large references the run's replies ran to about 21k characters, 8 times, against a median of 445. On a lid of 5 bodies over a fan, 15 pairs with one overlap and two boxes, the reply is 1,896 characters, the overlap's pair whole.
- **`light`'s `DISAGREE` names its two figures,** the method that gave each, and where: the pane's footprint, or the narrowest band's two ends, and what to render to look. On `suncatcher-3` "the narrowest band is 1.500 mm ..., rays across it find 0.987 mm" was read as the leading being too thick. Planted: a plate 0.1 mm thick tilted 5° over a pane, which the sections cut into strips a layer apart and rays meet everywhere.
- **`thin` says where each thin place is.** Its spots, a layer at a time, are joined into places where one layer's meets the next's, and up to the first 10, thinnest first, are said with their box, how many layers they run through and their least width across by rays (`thin_places`). On `suncatcher-2` the reply gave 40 spots and the thinnest, the positions were in the record, and the model took a note about a wedge for where they were. The verdict counts the places and the spots.
- **`capture` says what it reads each pane against:** where its underside came from (`--pane-floor-z`, the pocket's floor, the level face facing up in its opening nearest under `--pocket-z`, or the part's bottom where there is none), its top, and the level faces over and under its opening that are its rim, with their heights and areas (`panes.N.read_against`). A pane that misses `--pocket-z` says where its underside came from, and to give `--pane-floor-z`. On `eval-unnamed-2` "runs from z=0.000 to z=1.000, which misses --pocket-z" didn't say where 0 came from, and the model guessed the bench moved the pane.
- **`faces FILE`** reads a STEP part's flat faces, a plane at a time, largest first: the way it faces (`+z`, or its normal), where (`z=7.5`), its area, its box and size, and how many faces are in it; and each full cylinder, its split faces joined: a hole (its surface facing its axis) or a boss, its centre half way along its axis, the axis, radius and depth, from where to where, and for a hole whether each end is open, by rays along its axis. A part of a cylinder, such as a rounding, is counted and left to `edges`. Second readings: each plane's area by the fine mesh's triangles in it, within the chords round its curved edges; each cylinder by the mesh cut square to its axis half way along, a circle fitted to the cut's points round it (centre and radius within 0.005 mm), and its depth from the mesh's points on it. An STL has no faces, and is told to use `slice`. It replaces the run's scripts on a bought part's faces, a fan's holes and a plug's ends and feet. On a fan block 30 x 30 x 7.5 with four 3.4 mm holes it reads six planes and the four holes at their centres, open at both ends.
- **`slice FILE --at z=H`** (and `x=`, `y=`, more than once) cuts the part by a plane: each separate piece, largest first, with its area, its box in the part's axes, and its outline, said as a rectangle (and how it's turned) or a circle where it is one, its points where it has 12 or fewer once simplified, and else their count; and each hole, its centre, size, area and, where round, its diameter. The second reading is OCCT's section of the exact surface, its area, pieces and holes, or on an STL its triangles cut one by one. A plane that misses the part says where the part runs. It replaces the run's 9 cross-section scripts. Through the fan block at mid-height it reads one 30 mm square with four 3.4 mm holes, and upright through two holes, three pieces.
- **`slice` reads each piece's least width,** and a need can hold what a section reads (mqucifer/print-shop#101). `cuts.N.pieces.M.least_width_mm` is the thickness of the piece's thinnest part across: a straight wall's thickness, or a tab's. It's read from the polygon's edges, for each two that face each other within 30° and share no corner, as the least distance along one's inward normal to the other where they overlap, through the piece, so a sharp tip, where two sides meet, isn't a width; and a piece with no two such edges, a triangle, has none. The second reading, `least_width_rays_mm`, is rays from points 0.1 mm apart along each edge, its ends among them, along its inward normal to the first edge they meet, counted between the same pairs; they agree within 0.037 mm (the chords, and the rays' half-step where two facing edges close at 30°), or it's `DISAGREE`. `cuts.N.pieces_count` and `cuts.N.holes` count a level's pieces and holes, and `cuts.N.pieces.M.size_mm` is a piece's size along the plane's two axes. **A place named** by `--box NAME=X0,Y0,Z0,X1,Y1,Z1` reads, at each level through it, the section inside the box, `cuts.N.places.NAME`: its `pieces`, their least width and size, and `whole_pieces`, how many of the level's pieces lie wholly inside it, so a need reads one wall or one tab after a rebuild changes the pieces' order. A tab cut free is one whole piece in a box round it; one fused to its wall is none. `needs` takes these as a `section` measure (`docs/rounds.md`). Evidence: `eval-edges-5` sent a 2 mm wall need on `slice` that was refused, so the wall was held only by the script's `WALL = 2`; and a design run's tabs were fused to their wall for their whole height while the model wrote that they stood free (lessons runs9-edges:5 and design-runs:34). Planted (`slice/`): an open box 50 x 35 x 20 with 2 mm walls reads one ring at z 10, `least_width_mm` 2.000 by both methods; a wall with four tabs 1.2 mm thick cut 0.5 mm free of it reads 5 pieces at z 10, between their root and hook, the ring at 2.000 and each tab at 1.200, a box round one tab 1 piece wholly inside it; the same tabs fused read 1 piece, and the box none wholly inside.
- Both are readings, with no limit and no `FAIL`: what's there, or `DISAGREE` where the two readings differ. A need can name their numbers (`holes.0.radius_mm`, `cuts.0.pieces`), but a check of the part goes through the command that judges it.

**`edges`** (mqucifer/print-shop#60) reads each outside edge of a STEP part: the Sponsor wants a case's edges, corners and lid softened, and nothing read an edge.
- **An edge** is read at points along it, every 2 mm, at least 3 and at most 25: the angle its two faces' normals turn through. Under 1° it's smooth, where a rounding meets a face. Over it, it's sharp, and outside if a point a little way into each face, and then between the two, is in the part; otherwise it's an inside corner, and isn't read. A chamfer's two edges are sharp, each turning 45°.
- **A rounded edge** is a curved face that meets a neighbour smoothly, a fillet or a corner where fillets meet, whatever it was built as. Its radius is the least it bends through convexly, read on a 9 by 9 grid over the face. A sharp edge is radius 0, so `least_radius_mm` is 0 where one is read, and `least_rounded_mm` is the least of the rounded ones, each with where.
- **Outside is what a finger reaches:** a ball 12 mm across, the test finger of IEC 60529 (IPXX-B) and IEC 61032 (test probe B), from outside every file named, and touching the edge from between its faces, not only flat-on to one. So the inside of a closed case isn't read when its lid is named, a seat flush with the lid isn't, nor the rim of a slot too narrow for a fingertip, and an edge round a plug's opening is. Each layer of every file is sliced onto a grid (0.25 mm, coarser past 8 million cells), Euclidean distance transforms find where a ball's centre fits and which of those places join the outside, and each place a ball touches an edge is held clear of every file by the mesh's exact distance. Every file named blocks the ball; one under `references/`, or named with `--with`, isn't read.
- **`--min-radius R`** fails a sharp edge or a rounding under R, naming each with where. A rounding drawn at R passes: it may read short by 0.5% of R or 0.005 mm. With no limit it's a reading, and passes.
- **A rounding stored as flat strips** reads as sharp edges, each turning a few degrees: one under 20° is counted in `faceted_edges`, and the reply says to build it with `fillet` or an arc. Rounded 3 mm as 16 strips a corner, a slab reads 68 such edges and no rounded face.
- **Second checks:** at each sharp edge read (150 at most, spread over them), the fine mesh cut by a plane square to it, and the angle its outline turns through at the edge, within 1° and each chord's lean; at each rounded face's least radius, the mesh cut along the direction it bends most, and a circle fitted to the outline's points on that face within half the radius, within 0.02 mm and 3% of it; and the ball's centre the grid found for each touch point, held clear by the mesh's distance. That the centre got there from outside is the grid's alone.
- **By group and by place** (mqucifer/print-shop#65). On `eval-edges-1` the need on the four upright corners read `rounded.19.radius_mm`, the 20th rounding in a list sorted by radius, which a rebuild can reorder, and three long calls went on picking corners out of the list. Each sharp edge and rounding read is now in one group, in the file's axes: `upright` where it runs within 30° of z (a rounding's way is square to its normal and to where it bends most); else `top` or `bottom` where its outward direction, the mean of its faces' normals, is more than 20° above or below level; and `side` for the rest. A rounding that faces up or down is a top's or a bottom's whichever way it runs, as a ball's corner bends alike both ways. `numbers.groups.NAME` has each group's counts, its least radius (0 with a sharp edge in it, none with nothing), where and on which file, and a need names `groups.upright.least_radius_mm`. `--box NAME=X0,Y0,Z0,X1,Y1,Z1` takes the edges with any point read inside the box, said at a point inside it, and `--facing NAME=DIR` those facing within 60° of one of `+x -x +y -y +z -z`, the edges round the face that faces it, each as `numbers.selected.NAME`. A rounding's radius is its own least, wherever on it that is, so a box never reads one looser than it is. Each group's and selection's least is among those the second method checks, past the 150 spread over the rest. An opening's edges in a side wall are grouped by the way they face (its top edge faces down, so `bottom`): read them with `--box`. On `mixed`, upright reads 3 mm, top 1.5, bottom 0 with its 8 sharp edges, and side none.
- **Planted:** a block rounded 2 mm all over (`OK` at 2, `FAIL` at 2.1); one with its bottom left sharp (8 sharp edges, all at its foot); an open tray (16 sharp edges a finger meets, 12 inside corners not read), the same tray with its lid flush on it (none), and with the lid as a reference (none, the lid not read). On a case 100 by 70 by 35 mm with 30 vent slots, a grille of holes, four bosses and a plug's opening, it takes 9 to 11 s: the opening's 4 edges are read, and the slots' and holes' rims are flat-on.

**`rays` from a point in air** (mqucifer/print-shop#65). On `eval-edges-1` rays from a rounded corner's centre, which lay in the box's hollow, read `across` 5.66 to 53.92 mm, and it took three long calls to see that wasn't the corner's radius. Where the centre is in air, each level now gives `nearest_wall_mm`, the nearest wall any ray meets, and a note says what `across` is: the open space's width through the point, from the wall one ray meets to the wall the opposite ray meets, a hole's diameter on its axis, and never an outside corner's radius, whose centre is always inside the part, in its material or its hollow, with `edges` to read it. From the tray's corner centre, 1 mm from two inside walls, the nearest wall reads 1.0 and the least across 2√2.

**`wall`** answers what `thin`'s `thinnest_flat_mm` was quoted for on `cup-1`: the hanging hole's wall. Any point inside the hole names it, and it reads the hole's whole length. The sections take the least distance from the hole's edge to any other edge of the material round it. The rays never touch a section, and meet them where the wall is least, as the least wall runs square to the hole's wall. Between two corners it isn't square to either, rays read it thicker, and that's `DISAGREE`, said why. Material beyond the hole's ends isn't counted. `least_wall_mm` is the number a need limits, or `--min-wall`, which holds a wall drawn at the limit within the mesh's chords.

**A hole that opens into a hollow** (mqucifer/print-shop#136). In an eval, screw holes through a 2 mm floor at the bottom of a hollow channel read the channel's 1.6 mm side wall, `FAIL` twice: the hollow is a closed hole in each section too, so `wall` followed the hole up into it. The model fell back on `slice --at z=... --box`, and on a test piece rather than the part (lessons runs14-neon-mount:3 and :11). Now:
- **`--z FROM,TO`** reads only the sections within that stretch of the hole, along its axis (z, or the `--axis` given): `--z 0,2` reads the floor round the hole. The point still names the hole, and needn't be in the stretch; a stretch the hole has no section in is `NO ANSWER`, saying where the hole runs. A note says what isn't read, `hole_read` has the stretch, and `hole_runs` says where the hole itself runs.
- **Where the hole's section more than doubles from one layer to the next** (`OPENS`, 2: a 45-degree countersink on a 3 mm hole grows 1.3 times a layer), it opens into a hollow, or a counterbore, there. When what's read reaches into it, the verdict's line says so, where the least wall is, and the stretch that reads the hole alone, at the level face between the two layers: "FAIL: the least wall round the hole is 1.600 mm, ..., under the 2 mm asked for, ...; the hole opens into a hollow at 2 along z, and this reads its whole run, the hollow too: the least wall is at 2.300, in the hollow. To read the wall round the hole alone, give --z 0,2". An `OPENS INTO A HOLLOW` line gives each opening's sections, and `hole_opens_at` and `hole_alone` have them. So the model learns the flag from the bench's reply.
- **Without `--z` the reading is as it was**, and a hole that opens into nothing is said word for word as before.
- Planted (`wall/hollow-floor`): a box 20 x 20 x 12, hollowed from z 2 up with 1.6 mm walls, a 3 mm hole through its floor 3.5 mm from the outside. With `--min-wall 2` and no range, `FAIL` at 1.600 mm, the opening named at 2 (7.07 to 282.24 mm² in one layer) and `--z 0,2` given; with `--z 0,2`, `OK` at 3.500 mm by the sections and 3.5003 by the rays; `--z 3,5`, in the hollow, reads 1.600 and says so (`~/print-shop/proofs/m18-eval-fixes/`).

**A close-up** draws R mm all round a point along a view (`top` unless named), so a fine motif or a 3 mm hole can be judged: at 512 px and `R` of 4 that's 64 px per mm, where the first real run's 3 mm holes were 27 px. It looks along the view and doesn't cut anything, so what's inside a part needs `--section`. Nothing of the parts in the window is a `FAIL`, so one aimed wrongly doesn't read as a picture.

**A test piece** is drawn see-through in grey, and each picture says it isn't the part. It's whatever the one asking says it is, as nothing can tell by looking. So a script writes test pieces to files of their own, and a piece inside the part's file is drawn as the part.

**`capture`** answers whether the frame's shape holds each pane. It's how the Sponsor prints them, like a lens in a pair of sunglasses without the snap: a pane is set in at a pause and printed over to lock it in, not to bond it. So nothing here reads a material or counts on adhesion, and the frame's material is free. For each face of each pane:
- **Can it slide out?** The pane is read at every place its play lets it sit, against everything beyond the pocket, by polygons and then as a solid swept out. Passing at any of them is `FAIL`, so a pane held only while it's centred isn't held.
- **Can it swing out?** A lip that lies all to one side of the pane's centre is `FAIL`, as it can pivot about that lip.
- **How much lip?** `least_lip_mm` is the narrowest round the edge, and `least_lip_with_play_mm` what's left once the pane has moved away from it. Under `--min-lip` after its play (0.4 mm, a nozzle's width, unless you say) is `FAIL`, with how much of the edge and where.
- **How thick?** A lip under a layer at its root is `FAIL`, and `air_mm` is how far the pane can rattle before it reaches it.
- **Edge with no lip** is reported and not judged, as in `panes`, if the rest goes round. Where a lip stops it falls away to nothing round a window's corner, which isn't counted as thin, and one that tapers over a long way is.
- **Why it isn't held** (mqucifer/print-shop#46). On `eval-tip-1` the pocket's cut ran on through the top face, and `capture` said only that the pane comes out, with no lip along all 59.3 mm of its edge. The model's own 2D check said the lip was there, it judged the bench wrong, and it spent about 1.5 hours and four calls of 21k–40k tokens' reasoning that ended without a tool call. Now a face that comes out, or holds the pane by less than the limit along half its edge or more, says what the frame has beyond that face, stretch by stretch, with where on the edge and the heights it read. It reads every section beyond the pane's face as one, along the line square to the pane's edge at each spot, and says one of:
  - the frame covers the pane by only so much, where the limit after play needs more;
  - nothing covers the edge, and the frame over the pane starts so far in from it;
  - the hole is as wide as the pocket, to 0.03 mm, so nothing overhangs the pane on that face and the pocket's cut may run through the face;
  - the hole is so much wider than the pocket, or narrower but stopping short of the pane, and the frame starts so far out from the pane's edge;
  - there's no frame within 10 mm, or nothing of the frame beyond the pane's face at all, with the pane's face and the frame's end.

  The pocket's wall is read on the same line, from the opening at `--pocket-z`. Frame met across a pane counts as over a spot only if it's no nearer the pane's edge anywhere else, so the far edge's lip isn't read as this one's. Each kind is said once, with how much of the edge it runs along, in how many stretches, where the longest runs, and the range of its figures. A single spot (a lip's falling end, a corner) isn't said, and every stretch is in each face's `over`. **Second check:** at a spot in each stretch, rays straight beyond the face, walked along the same line, and the pocket's wall by a level ray at `--pocket-z`. A different kind, or a figure more than 0.04 mm off, is `DISAGREE`. Rebuilt from `eval-tip-1`'s frame with its pocket cut through the top again, the reply is the run's own at call 87, then "Over it, the frame read from z=2.5 to its top at z=4: all round its edge, 59.3 mm, the hole is as wide as the pocket" (`~/print-shop/proofs/m18-why/`).

**A rim sized to the visible share** (mqucifer/print-shop#37, criterion 6). On `suncatcher-2` the Sponsor ruled 1.2 mm for big panes and 0.55 mm for small ones, before play, and a need could only give one `--min-lip` for all, so nothing held the big panes at 1.2 mm. "Small" by name or by eye can't be checked: the Sponsor, 2026-11-04, it has to be a math check, not a matter of knowing. `--full-rim R --least-rim R2 --min-visible P` take the request's full rim and smallest rim, before play, from the pocket's wall to the opening's edge, and its least share of each pane visible. All three go together, with no default, and not with `--min-lip`.
- **The rim each pane needs:** the full rim if P% of the pane stays visible with it; if not, the widest rim that keeps P%, by insetting the pane's outline (true distance, round joins) by the rim less the play and bisecting; and if even the smallest rim can't keep P%, none, and the pane is to be removed. A long thin pane is judged by how much its rims cover: its width counts and its length doesn't.
- **The check:** the pane's lip, read per spot as for `--min-lip`, is at least the rim it needs all round, and its visible share on the frame as built, covered on neither face, is at least P%. The visible share is `light`'s own reading (`glass.visible`), one function for both.
- **Tolerances:** the lip may fall 0.01 mm short of the rim it needs, the mesh's chords on both edges, and the share 0.5 percentage points short. A rim drawn exactly at the need leaves exactly the share, so the share's tolerance is the same chords read as area.
- **What it says:** each pane's share, the share wanted, the rim it needs and the rim it has, and how that rim was chosen, in three figures (mqucifer/print-shop#61): the full rim, the share it leaves visible, and the smaller rim that keeps the share wanted, or that even the smallest rim doesn't. On `suncatcher-3` the reply gave the rim to set and not how, and two calls of long reasoning rebuilt the logic. `rim.chosen` is `full`, `smaller` or `remove`. A failing pane says "set this pane's rim to X mm before play", with where it's short, or "remove this pane", or, where its rim is right and too little shows, that the frame covers it elsewhere.
- **The rim to set is the rim needed, rounded down to 0.001 mm,** so a cell built at it passes in one step: rounded down, the share stays at least the one wanted, and the lip falls short of the need by under 0.001 mm, inside its tolerance. Rounded to 0.01 mm, a 2.71 mm pane needing 0.5554 mm would be told 0.56, which leaves 59.48% visible, and be told 0.56 again. On `suncatcher-2`'s ask-2 frame rebuilt with the skill's mitred offsets at each figure the reply gave, every pane passes at the next `capture` (`~/print-shop/proofs/m18-verify/one-step-ask2/`).
- **Second checks:** the share by rays straight up through the frame from a grid in the pane, and the share at the rim needed by a grid of points at least that far from the pane's edge.
- **Which way the rim moves** (mqucifer/print-shop#65). - **Back-tested** on `suncatcher-2`'s ask-2 frame at 1.2 mm, 0.55 mm and 60%, against the operator's own inset of each pane file: the rim needed agrees to 0.001 mm and the share at the full rim to 0.00 points. The big panes need 1.2 mm, the legs 0.78, the triangles 0.86 and L1 and R1 0.99, and none is removed (`~/print-shop/proofs/m18-verify/share-ask2/`).
- **Rims and lips:** a rim is a lip before play, from the pocket's wall. `least_lip_mm` is the lip after play, how far it covers the pane in the middle of its pocket. `--min-lip` takes the play off once more, for a pane slid to one side, as it always has, and keeps its 0.4 mm when given alone.

A pane too thick for its pocket is `FAIL`, as is a height that misses the pane. It doesn't read a pane forced past a lip that bends. Pair it with `pause`, which says when the pane goes in and whether the nozzle clears it.

## Never take a tool's word

The map's table, row by row. Each row that touches these commands is a planted case in `tests/test_bench.py`, named after it.

Building this found more, each now handled and said in the code:
- **CadQuery's `tessellate` tolerance is relative,** a share of each edge's size, not the mm it looks like. On a real part that read as a 0.03 mm false gap. The bench meshes with absolute tolerances, through OCCT directly.
- **Manifold's `RayHit.distance` is a fraction of the ray,** not a length.
- **OCCT's adaptive volume moves with its accuracy setting** on a real file by far more than its own error estimate, so the bench reads its default, and checks it against the mesh.
- **A coarser mesh isn't always further from the truth,** so how far a figure can rest on the chords comes from the chord tolerance times the curved surface's area.

A second check has to ask the same question as the first, or it only makes noise. Running over our 51 reference parts showed that: rays along a surface's normal read a cone or a thread thinner than it prints, and the top of a cylinder on its side as thin when no layer prints it. So a wall's ray runs level, across the layer, and shallow slopes aren't read as walls. A sharp point, a feather edge, a cusp and a few stray rays are noted with where they are, and don't fail a part.

**What holds a bought part** (mqucifer/print-shop#76). - **The hold.** The first distance at which the part meets another, `slide.first_mm`, which parts, and the box of each first contact, read 0.02 mm past it, with the named `--box` it lies in, or "outside every named box". - **The path.** Every part the slide meets before it's clear: its box, each piece of it (each hook), and `reach_mm`, how far in it reaches from the edge of the moving part's outline, seen along the slide. **`info` and `readback` on an STL: what the second check compares** (mqucifer/print-shop#144). The mesh's volume is the divergence theorem over its triangles. The second is 128 plane sections, each one's area by its polygons times the height it stands for. The two agree within 0.5% of the mesh's volume, the stack's own error over a slope or a curve (`STACK_SHARE`), or 0.001 mm³; past it, `DISAGREE`. A section at a step's middle stands for the whole step, which is wrong where the section changes all at once: at a level face inside the step. On a given channel letter, a tray 25 mm tall with a 2 mm floor, the floor's top lay 0.047 mm into a step, which read the walls' area where it was still floor: 59683.300 mm³ against the mesh's 60135.386, 0.75% apart, `DISAGREE` on a plain watertight body, and every need on `info` or `readback` for a part cut from it failed (lesson runs14-neon-mount:14). So the sections are stacked between level faces: each stretch between two heights the mesh has a level face at gets its share of the 128, one at least, and no step holds a level face. A stretch with upright sides is then exact, and the tolerance is for what's left. The reply says so: "second check: volume by sections: 11171.2693 mm³, 128 sections stacked between its level faces, within 0.5% of the mesh's (55.856 mm³)". A body with no level face inside it, such as a sphere, reads as before. Planted (`given/`): a tray like that channel, `DISAGREE` before at 11076.130 against 11171.269, now `OK` with both 11171.269; and two cubes in one mesh, one into the other, watertight, whose mesh counts their shared 350 mm³ twice: 2000.000 against the sections' 1650.000, `DISAGREE` before and now. ## The way on, and the bend

(mqucifer/print-shop#142; M18's "physics as projects need it".) A design run approved a snap-on lid that could never be fitted. `clearance --slide` read it held once seated, and nothing read the way on, where each clip had to bend several times what printed PETG takes across its layers. The Sponsor caught it by eye before printing, and said (2026-11-15): "We need to use tools at our disposal and not do everything with a single shot harness". FreeCAD, tried headless on that lid (mqucifer/print-shop#15), read both, and Fusion's joint and interference agreed with it step for step on the way on. So these two commands call FreeCAD 1.1.3, with gmsh 4.15.2 and CalculiX 2.23, in an image of its own under the bench's rules, and report as the others do. No need has to name them. **The harness runs both itself when a run says it's done** (mqucifer/print-shop#150, [`docs/rounds.md`](rounds.md#when-a-run-says-its-done-the-way-on-and-the-bend-mquciferprint-shop150)): each held part's way on, and the arm at each place it passes, found from the place by `bench/arm.py`.

**When to use which.**

| The question | The command |
|---|---|
| Is the part held once it's on, and by what? | `clearance HOLDER PART --slide <its way out>` |
| Can it get on, and how far does what's in its way have to move? | `fit-on PART --onto HOLDER --along <its way out> --from MM` |
| Does the arm that has to move survive it? | `bend HOLDER --arm BOX --root PLANE --push <fit-on's depth> --toward DIR` |

**`fit-on`** (`bench/fit_on.py`). MOVING is the part's file where it sits once on. `--along` is its way out, as `--slide` takes it (`z` is `+z`), and `--from` how far out it starts: far enough to be clear. `--from clear` is as far as it takes for its box to be past every other file's along the way, and a step more, as a whole number of steps (`from_clear`, mqucifer/print-shop#150): 8.25 mm for the planted lid, whose skirt's foot is 8 mm under the box's rim. It's placed at each `--step` (0.25 mm) from there to where it sits, and at each step intersected with each `--onto` file.
- **Each piece of overlap** has its volume, its box, `height_mm` along the way, and its size seen along the way: `depth_mm`, its least width across, the direction that's along (`across`), and `along_mm`, its length the other way. Across a wall the part passes, the depth is how far the wall, or the arm on it, has to move aside: the bend. **The bench doesn't know which way anything bends,** so it gives the direction, and a piece narrower along its wall than it's deep reads the narrower way.
- **Places.** Pieces that lie over each other from step to step are one place, said once, deepest first (`places.N`): the step where it's deepest, and the stretch of the way it lasts (`first_mm` to `last_mm`). Its deepest step is then looked for between the steps, halving down to 0.02 mm, so a hook with a sloped face isn't read only where a step happens to land.
- **Verdict:** `CLEAR` when nothing overlaps at any step, exit 0. `BLOCKED` otherwise, exit 1, naming the deepest place, with `depth_mm` the deepest of all. A snap fit is meant to be `BLOCKED`: the reply gives the `bend` command with that depth as its `--push`. A need on one names the verdict, `["BLOCKED"]`, and holds `depth_mm` with `at_most`.
- **An overlap where the part sits** (`seated`) is said as that: no bend clears it, and `clearance` reads it.
- **Second method:** rays along the way on, through a mesh of each part at 0.005 mm, with no boolean. The part only moves along the rays, so one set reads every step: each ray's stretches inside the moving part slide by the step, and what they share with its stretches inside the other part is that step's overlap. Rays 0.1 mm apart over the whole outline (further apart on a part too large for 400,000 of them) look at every step for an overlap two rays or more across that `common` didn't find. Rays 0.02 mm apart over each place read its depth again, as the least width of the rays inside both parts. A depth more than twice the rays' spacing and 0.01 mm apart, a place the rays don't find, or an overlap only the rays find is `DISAGREE`.
- **Planted** (`tests/cae_cases/make_fixtures.py`, `snap/`): an open box 60 x 40 x 30 with one clip inside its front wall, an arm 8 mm wide and 1.2 mm thick, free for 16 mm, its hook 2 mm tall and 0.8 mm out; and a lid whose skirt comes down 0.2 mm clear of the arm's face, with a window the hook snaps into. The band under the window passes the hook from 4 mm out down to 0.4: `BLOCKED`, 0.600 mm deep across y by `common` and 0.600 by the rays, 8.000 mm along, at 14 of 25 readings, 3.75 mm out down to 0.5. With the clip 0.8 mm further back, `CLEAR`. With the hook a wedge, read at 5, 2.5 and 0 mm out, the one step that meets it reads 0.4 mm and the deepest, 0.600, is found at 1.25 mm out. With the walls 0.5 mm too tall, the lid's plate is in the rim where it sits, 192 mm³, and the reply names no push. Each ran in under 2 s alone.

**`bend`** (`bench/bend.py`). `--arm` is a box round the arm, hook and all, one side of it on the arm's root, where it joins the rest, and clear of the wall beside it. The arm is the file's material in the box. It's held at `--root` (`z=10`), and one edge is pushed `--push` mm `--toward` a way across it.
- **An edge, not a face.** What pushes a hook meets it along a line and leaves it free to tilt. Holding a whole face square stops it tilting and doubles the strain at the root (the trial, mqucifer/print-shop#15). The edges a push can meet are those along the arm's extreme on the side the push comes from, as a hook's flat face or its crest has. `--at far` is the one furthest from the root, `near` the nearest, and `both`, the default, pushes each in turn and judges the worse: the nearer edge is the shorter arm, which the usual formula's length misses. A rounded hook has no such edge, and is `NO ANSWER`.
- **It's linear and static,** so the strain is in proportion to the push whatever the material's stiffness: only Poisson's ratio, 0.38, is the material's. The mesh is second-order tetrahedra of at most half the arm's thickness at its root, on one thread, so the same arm gives the same figure every time.
- **What's judged** is the strain across the layers, the stretch along `--layers` (z unless said), which is what pulls one printed layer off the next: its peak anywhere on the arm but within half a thickness of the pushed edge, where a push along a line has no finite strain in any mesh (`pushes.far.peak_pct`, and `across_layers_pct` for the worse push). The reply also has it on the root and within a thickness above it, the largest stretch any way, and where each is.
- **The limit is the material's across its layers,** not its bulk strength (decided with the Sponsor, 2026-11-15). `--material petg`, the only one so far, is 1%: Prusament PETG's datasheet (Prusa Polymers, version 1.1, 16-02-2022) gives "Interlayer Adhesion [MPa] 18 ± 4" and a tensile modulus of 1.5 ± 0.1 GPa, so layers part at about 18 / 1500 = 1.2% strain, 0.9% at the low end of both; in the plane of the layers the same sheet gives 5.1% at yield. Another material is added with its source, and `--limit PCT` gives the request's own. Nothing here has been compared with a print.
- **The room behind the arm.** `back_moves_mm` is how far the arm's back moves toward the push, and `room_behind_mm` how far behind it the rest of the part is, read by sweeping the back faces along the push into what the box left. An arm that meets what's behind it before the push is done can't bend as this reads it: `FAIL`, saying both figures.
- **Verdict:** `OK`, or `FAIL` over the limit or short of room.
- **What would pass** (`would_pass`, mqucifer/print-shop#150). A strip's strain is ε = 1.5·t·y/L², in proportion to its thickness and its push and to one over its length squared. So from the reading's own peak, each with the other two as they are: at this push, the most thickness at the root (`thickness_mm`, t × limit / peak) and the least length from the root to the pushed edge (`length_mm`, L × √(peak / limit)); and at this arm, the most push (`push_mm`, y × limit / peak). The reply says all three beside what the arm has, and the room it would need where it's short of that. The push is exact, as the solve is linear: the short clip reads 2.98% pushed 0.6 mm, `would_pass.push_mm` is 0.201, and pushed that it reads 0.998%. The thickness and the length are the strip's law applied to an arm that isn't quite a strip, so a changed arm is read again. With `DISAGREE` there's no one figure to scale, and none is given.
- **Second method:** a beam, by hand. The arm is cut into 40 slabs from its root to the pushed edge, each slab's area moment and outer fibre from OCCT's volume properties; the tip's force comes from the push by summing each slab's give, and a slab's strain from its moment. On a plain strip that's ε = 1.5·t·y/L², which the reply says too, with t and L read off the arm. The two are held together half way along the arm, by the strain along it, where neither has the root's corner or the push in it. Past 20% apart it's `DISAGREE`. A wide arm bends partly as a plate, up to 1/(1 − ν²), 17%, stiffer where it can't curl across its width, and a beam has no shear: on the planted clips the two are 7 to 11% apart, and the solve's peak is 0.99 to 1.08 times the beam's root figure.
- **Planted:** the clip above, pushed 0.6 mm at its hook's top edge, 16 mm from its root: 0.455% by the solve, where ε = 1.5·t·y/L² is 0.422%, 8% apart, at a 0.6 mm mesh. At its foot, 14 mm from the root, 0.590% against 0.551%, and that's the one judged: `OK` within 1%, with 1.000 mm behind it. Both pushes take under 6 s alone. Pushed 1.6 mm, 1.21%: `FAIL`. Half as long, 1.757% at its top edge: `FAIL`. In a box with 0.45 mm behind it, `FAIL` on the room, its strain the same. Pushed sideways, 8 mm thick and 16 long, the two are 24 and 34% apart: `DISAGREE`. At a 0.3 mm mesh the judged figure is 0.61%, against 0.59% at 0.6.

**`bench/arm.py`** isn't a command: it's the harness's, run in the second image, and finds the arm at a place `fit-on` read, as a box, a root plane and a push for `bend` (mqucifer/print-shop#150; how, and when it finds none, is in [`docs/rounds.md`](rounds.md#when-a-run-says-its-done-the-way-on-and-the-bend-mquciferprint-shop150)). On the planted box it gives the box the tests give `bend` by hand, `-4.1,-17.1,10` to `4.1,-14.9,26.1`, root `z=10`, toward `-y`; on the lid's band, held at both ends, none.

**What neither reads:** friction, how hard the part has to be pushed, how well a snug fit or a snap holds, a way on that isn't one straight line, an arm that yields or bends so far its shape changes the load (the solve is the first, linear answer, with no contact), and how the print's direction weakens a part other than through the one limit across its layers. Something thinner along the way than a step, passing something as thin, can be stepped over: give a smaller `--step`. Every figure above is in `~/print-shop/proofs/m18-fit-on/planted/`.

## Joined parts under a weight, and each part against the bed

(mqucifer/print-shop#156, mqucifer/print-shop#168 and mqucifer/print-shop#170; M20's side table, in mqucifer/project-print `ROADMAP.md`.) A side table is bigger than the bed, so it's printed as parts and joined, with bought bolts, nuts and washers where it takes load: printed parts are held mechanically, never by glue, by through-bolts (decided with the Sponsor, 2026-11-18). A leg is taller than the bed, so it's split too, and its pieces join with a tapered dovetail and a printed click latch; glue goes in channels in the flanks as a second hold only (the Sponsor, the same day: "We can glue, it just can't be the primary hold"). `bend` reads one arm pushed aside. Nothing read several parts joined together with a weight on them, or a set of parts against the bed. The table's load is read with FreeCAD (decided with the Sponsor at M20's planning session, 2026-11-17), as M18 reads the way on and the bend: a tool we have, not the harness. `load` runs in the second image as it is, `print-shop/cae:0.1`, with no new package, and `fit-bed` in the first.

**When to use which.**

| The question | The command |
|---|---|
| Does each part of a split fit the bed, and on which side? | `fit-bed assembly.json`, or `fit-bed a.step b.step` |
| Do the joined parts hold the weight, the way each is printed? | `load assembly.json --weight KG --at X,Y,Z` |
| How far does the top move under it? | the same reply's `moves_mm` |
| What does each joint have to carry? | the same reply's `joints.NAME` |
| Does a through-bolt hold, and what gives first: the bolt, the plastic under a washer or the nut, or the plastic round the hole? | the same reply's `joints.NAME.gives_first` and `nearest_share` |
| Does each nut's pocket fit its nut? | `fit-bed assembly.json`, `pockets.NAME` |
| Does a split leg's dovetail hold with no glue counted: which way does the load drive its tail, and what holds it? | `load`'s reply, `joints.NAME.drive_n`, `faces` and `latch` |
| Lifted by its top, does anything drop off? | `load assembly.json --lift top` |
| Can a dovetail be put together the way the file says, and does its latch bend far enough to get on? | `load`'s reply, `ways_in.NAME`, read before anything is solved (mqucifer/print-shop#184 and mqucifer/print-shop#185); `clearance SOCKET TAIL --slide <way>`, `fit-on` and `bend` read the same by hand |

### The assembly file

Both read one file, JSON, written by whoever designs the parts (`bench/assembly.py`):

```json
{"parts": [
   {"name": "top", "file": "top.step", "at": [0, 0, 500], "material": "petg", "layers": "z"},
   {"name": "leg1", "file": "leg.step", "at": [10, 10, 0], "turn": [["z", 90]], "layers": "x"}],
 "joints": [
   {"between": ["top", "leg1"], "kind": "through", "bolt": "m5-8.8", "nut": "m5-nut",
    "washer": "m5-washer", "at": [[30, 30, 500]], "pocket": 0.2}],
 "held": [{"plane": "z=0", "how": "standing"}]}
```

- **`parts`**, each one solid in a STEP file beside the assembly file:
  - `name`, its own, which the reply and a need's path use (`parts.top.stretch_pct`), and `file`;
  - `at`, where it sits, `[x, y, z]` in mm, after each of `turn`: `[axis, degrees]` about the origin's axes, in order. A part drawn where it sits gives neither;
  - `material`, `petg` unless given, the only one so far;
  - `layers`, the axis of its own file its layers are stacked along: the way up on the bed. It turns with the part. There's no default: a printed part is weaker across its layers, so the file says how each is printed.
- **`joints`**, each `between` two parts that meet face to face, with its `kind`:
  - `through` (mqucifer/print-shop#168): a bolt right through both parts. A `bolt`, a `nut` and a `washer` from the tables below, all one thread, or the file is refused; and `at`, where each bolt is, a point on its axis. The bolt's head, on a washer, is on the first part named, and its nut on the second: on a washer too, or, with `pocket`, in a pocket there that holds it by its flats, with no washer under it. `pocket` is the play between each of the nut's flats and its pocket's wall, in mm. It takes no `insert` and no `pull_n`: what it holds is read;
  - `dovetail` (mqucifer/print-shop#170): a tail on the first part named, in a socket in the second. `slides` is the way the tail goes into its socket, toward its seat, one of `+x -x +y -y +z -z` in the assembly's axes. `load` reads it against the parts themselves before anything else, and a joint the parts can't take that way fails, naming the way they do (mqucifer/print-shop#185). `latch`, where it has one, is the flexing tab that holds it there: `{"on": PART, "arm": [[x0, y0, z0], [x1, y1, z1]], "root": "z=30"}`, the part the tab is on, a box round the tab, hook and all, with one side on its root, and the root's plane, as `bend` takes an arm, in the assembly's coordinates. **It takes nothing else.** Glue channels are the parts' own shape and count as nothing: no field gives glue, a bolt or the joint a figure, and a file that gives one is refused. `slides` and `latch` on any other kind are refused too, so `bonded` can't stand in for a dovetail;
  - `bonded`: glued or welded over the faces that meet. Printed parts aren't held this way (the Sponsor, 2026-11-18: "It needs some mechanical hold"), and `load` reads a bonded joint that carries anything as `UNREAD`;
  - `bolted`: a `bolt` and an `insert` from the tables below, and `at`, where each bolt is, a point on its axis. Its own figure for what each bolt holds is `pull_n`, in N, with its `source`, the words that say where it's from;
  - `touching`: held by nothing. It can push, and can't pull.
- **`held`**, where the assembly stands or is fixed: each a `plane` (`z=0`) and `how`, `standing` on it, held square to the plane and free to slide, or `fixed` to it, as a bracket is to a wall; and `part`, to hold one part's faces only. With none, it stands on its lowest level.
- **Parts meet face to face.** Two that pass through each other, two that meet with no joint named, a joint whose parts don't meet, and a held plane with no flat face in it are each `NO ANSWER`, saying where and by how much, and nothing is solved.

**The bought parts**, each figure with where it's from (`assembly.BOLTS`, `assembly.NUTS`, `assembly.WASHERS`, `assembly.INSERTS`):

- **How they were read** (2026-11-18): ISO's own text is sold, so each table was read from copies of it, two at least that agree on every figure here, saved with their addresses in `~/print-shop/proofs/m20-through/sources/`. Two other pages each differed from those in one figure not used here (one nut's least height, one nut's height), and aren't relied on.
- **A bolt's width** is its thread's name: M5 is 5 mm (`assembly.DIAMETER`).
- **A nut's own thread isn't judged.** ISO 898-2 pairs a class 8 nut with a class 8.8 bolt so that the pair can be tightened to the bolt's proof load without the thread stripping (its clause on the design of bolt and nut assemblies, for M5 to M39), and a bolt here is held to its proof load. M3 and M4 are outside that clause.

**What printed PETG takes pressed** (`assembly.PRESS`, mqucifer/print-shop#168). Under a washer, a nut or a bolt's shank the plastic is pressed, and Prusament PETG's datasheet (version 1.1, 16-02-2022) has no compressive figure: the word isn't on it. So a pressure is held to the same sheet's figures for a pull, which stand in for it, and every reply says so:
- **pressed across its layers,** which pushes them together: 45 MPa, the low end of "Tensile Yield Strength [MPa] 47 ± 2" for a specimen printed flat (ISO 527-1);
- **pressed along its layers,** which spreads them sideways and so pulls one off the next: 14 MPa, the low end of "Interlayer Adhesion [MPa] 18 ± 4", the figure `bend`'s 1% across the layers comes from.
- **Why a pull's figure stands in on the safe side** (the operator's ruling on mqucifer/print-shop#169): a thermoplastic yields pressed at no less than it yields pulled, so holding a pressure to the pull's figure doesn't pass what the plastic wouldn't take. That's the reasoning the stand-in rests on, with no figure of its own from a source, and both figures are taken at the low end of the sheet's range besides.
- A push is across the layers where it's nearer the way they're stacked than their plane. Nothing here has been compared with a print, and a crush test on the Sponsor's own PETG would be the figure to trust.

**PETG's density** (`assembly.DENSITY`, mqucifer/print-shop#170): 1.27 g/cm³. Prusament PETG's datasheet (Prusa Polymers, version 1.1, last update 16-02-2022), in its table "Typical material properties": "Density [g/cm3] 1.27", by ISO 1183. The sheet, as fetched on 2026-11-18 from the address `~/print-shop/proofs/m20-through/sources/README.md` gives, and its text are in `~/print-shop/proofs/m20-dovetail/sources/`. A part's own weight is its solid volume at that. A printed part has walls and infill and weighs less, so the solid weight is the careful one to hang from a latch.

**An insert's pull-out has no source yet.** A brass insert melted into printed PETG pulls out of its plastic long before a steel bolt stretches, so the insert's figure is the one that matters, and none is in the table. The figures found on 2026-11-17 were for other plastics (a test in nylon), an average across several plastics with no figure for PETG, or a claim with no method, and the two pages that might have given more couldn't be read. **So a bolted joint with no figure is `UNREAD`, never `OK` on its bolt alone** (the operator's ruling on mqucifer/print-shop#160): where a bolt has any pull to hold, and neither its insert nor its joint has a figure, the answer names the joint and the insert as unread, exit 2, and no need passes on it. A joint gives its own `pull_n` and `source`, from the request or a pull test, and is then read against the least of that and its bolt. A pull test on the Sponsor's own PETG and inserts is the figure to trust, and is the operator's to put to the Sponsor when the table's run is near.

### `load`

(`bench/load.py`.) `--weight` kg is shared equally between the places `--at` names, each on the assembly's surface, within 0.5 mm, and each spread over the surface within `--over` mm of it (10 unless given: a cup's foot, not a point). It pulls `--toward -z`, down, unless given.

- **`--lift PART`** is a load case of its own (mqucifer/print-shop#170), in place of `--weight` and `--at`: the assembly lifted by that part, as a table is by its top. It's held `fixed` by the flat faces at that part's highest level, against `--toward`, and by nothing else: the file's own `held` is set aside, and the reply says so. Every other part hangs by its own weight, `--toward` the way down: its solid's volume at its material's density, in N by standard gravity, 9.80665 m/s². It's put on every node of that part's own elements, a fifth of each element's share on each mid-side node less a twentieth on each corner, which is a uniform pull on a second-order tetrahedron, scaled so the part's total is its solid's and not its mesh's. `loads.N` has each part's `volume_mm3` and `grams`, `force_n` is the total, and `lift` the part named. The lifted part's own weight is in the hand, and isn't put on.

- **One body, solved once.** The parts are put in their places and joined by FreeCAD's `generalFuse`, so the faces that meet are shared and gmsh meshes both sides of each joint with the same nodes. CalculiX solves it linear and static through the FEM workbench, on second-order tetrahedra of at most half the thinnest part, and no finer than leaves about 40,000 of them, on one thread, so the same parts give the same figures every time. Where a curved side would turn an element inside out, as on a bolt's hole far smaller than the mesh, gmsh straightens it (its high-order optimisation, mqucifer/print-shop#168): without that CalculiX solved nothing for a table-sized top with 5.5 mm holes at a 10 mm mesh. Where it still does, the reply says an element is turned inside out and to give a finer `--mesh`. The weight is put on as a force on each node of the surface's triangles in the patch, and the reply says the patch's area and its middle, which is where the weight acts: a patch cut off by an edge isn't round.
- **Standing** holds each face in the plane square to it only, so a table's feet are free to spread, as on a floor: that's the more careful reading of the joints at the tops of its legs. Two nodes stop the rest sliding and turning, and carry nothing under a load square to the plane; a load along a standing plane is refused, saying to hold a plane `fixed`.
- **Each part** (`parts.NAME`):
  - `across_layers_pct`, the most it's pulled across its layers, the stretch along the way its layers are stacked, and `across_at`, where. It's held to the material's limit across its layers. A push across the layers parts nothing, so only a pull counts: a leg printed standing and pushed straight down passes.
  - `stretch_pct`, its largest stretch any way, a pull or a push (`stretch_is`), `stretch_at`, and `stretch_from_layers_deg`, how far out of its layers' plane it points: 0° is along them and 90° straight across. It's held to the material's limit in its layers' plane.
- **The limits are the material's, and the reply names their source,** as `bend` does. `petg`: Prusament PETG's datasheet (Prusa Polymers, version 1.1, 16-02-2022). Across the layers, 1%, as `bend` takes it: "Interlayer Adhesion [MPa] 18 ± 4" over a tensile modulus of 1.5 ± 0.1 GPa is about 1.2% strain, 0.9% at the low end of both. In the layers' plane, 5.1%, the same sheet's elongation at yield for a part printed flat. The stiffness is that modulus, 1500 MPa, and Poisson's ratio 0.38, `bend`'s. How far anything moves is in proportion to one over the stiffness, and the strains are too, as the load is a force: a sparse part is softer than this.
- **Each joint** (`joints.NAME`, the two parts' names joined by `+`): the force and the moment the second part puts on the first, about the middle of the faces that meet, as `force_n` and `moment_nmm`; and of those, `pull_n` square to the faces (a pull is more than 0, a push less), `shear_n` along them, `bending_nmm` and `twist_nmm`.
  - **It's read from the nodes' own balance,** not the surface's stress (`load.element_forces`): at each node of the faces that meet, the force the first part's own elements carry, each element's stiffness times how far its nodes moved, less what's put on that part there from outside. CalculiX balances exactly that sum at every node, so a part's joints add up to what's on it. The first reading summed the stress on the faces, and at a sharp inside corner, where a leg meets the underside of a top, it was up to 32% low: four legs carried 20.1 N of a 29.4 N weight between them. They now carry 7.34, 7.37, 7.34 and 7.36 N, 29.4 in all.
  - **Every joint is solved as one piece,** the faces tied all over, as a glued joint is and as a bolted one is while its bolts hold. What differs is how each is judged.
  - **Bolted:** what each bolt has to hold (`bolts`, and `bolt_pull_n` for the worst). The joint's pull is shared between its bolts. Its bending moment, less what a push through the faces' middle takes off it, is taken about the faces' edge on the pushed side, each bolt in proportion to its distance from that edge. The worst is held to `holds_n`: the least of the bolt's proof load, the insert's pull-out where one has a source, and the joint's own `pull_n` (`holds_from` says which). With neither an insert's figure nor the joint's, and a pull to hold, the joint is unread (`insert_unread`). The shear is shared between the bolts and said, and isn't judged: where an insert bears sideways on its plastic has no figure. A bolted joint's faces are in one plane.
  - **Through** (mqucifer/print-shop#168, `bench/load_through.py`): a bolt right through both parts, with nothing that depends on an insert. Its bolts' pulls are found as a bolted joint's are, and it's judged by the worst of three, for every bolt, in both parts:
    - **the bolt,** its pull against its proof load (`bolt_pull_n`, `holds_n`);
    - **the plastic pressed:** under each washer, or under the nut where it sits in a pocket, the bolt's pull over what bears there (`bolts.N.ends.K.pressure_mpa`, `bears_mm2`), and where the shank leans on the hole's wall, the shear over the bolt's width times the hole's length (`wall_pressure_mpa`), each against what the plastic takes pressed that way to its layers. What bears is the ring from the hole, or the washer's own hole where that's wider, out to the washer's outside, or to the nut's bearing face, cut to the part's own flat faces at the hole's end. A washer that isn't wholly on the part fails, saying how much of it is;
    - **the plastic round the hole,** read by the FEM as strain, as any part is (`bolts.N.ends.K.round`). The part alone is cut off two diameters of what bears on it from the bolt's axis and held where it's cut. The bolt's pull is spread over what the washer or the nut bears on, toward the other part, and its shear is put on the half of the hole's wall the shank leans on, most where it leans square. It's solved on a mesh of its own, and read within three quarters of the cut, as the hold strains what's beside it: pulled across the layers against the material's limit there, and the largest stretch any way against its limit in their plane. A hole near an edge leaves a thin bridge of plastic that the shank stretches, and that's where it tears out.
    - **The reply names what's nearest its limit** (`gives_first`), and by what share (`nearest_share`, 1 at the limit): it's never `OK` on the bolt alone. A joint whose bolts have nothing to hold, pushed together and not sheared, says so.
    - **Before anything is solved,** each bolt's way through is read from the parts' own faces (`bench/through.py`): a round hole in each part along the bolt's axis, within 0.05 mm of the place named, no narrower than the bolt, the two meeting end to end where the parts meet, each ending on a flat face square to the bolt. One without is `NO ANSWER`, saying which part and why.
    - **Second methods:** each bolt's pull on the coarser mesh and by statics, and the pressures from them, `DISAGREE` where one lands on the other side of a limit; and the plastic round each hole again on a mesh half as coarse again.
    - **What a through joint's reading leaves out:** it's read with its bolts snug and no more, so a bolt tightened hard, which presses its washers on the plastic by its own preload, isn't in it, and nor is PETG creeping under that; the pressure is taken as even under each washer; all the shear is taken by the shanks, none by friction, and a twist about the faces' normal by none; the bolt's length and its nut's thread aren't checked.
  - **Dovetail** (mqucifer/print-shop#170, `bench/dovetail.py` and `bench/load_dovetail.py`): a tail slid into a socket, held by its own shape.
    - **Whether it can be put together** (`ways_in.NAME`, `bench/way_in.py`, mqucifer/print-shop#184 and mqucifer/print-shop#185). eval-table-1's leg split read `OK` slid in along z, and its plan called it verified, though the socket's mouth was narrower than the tail's tip and the only way in was sideways.       - **The way out.** The tail's part is slid out of its socket against `slides` until its box is past the socket's part's. Each of its faces is drawn out along that way into a prism, and each prism is cut with the socket's part (OCCT's `common`), so what a face passes on the way out is what it has to pass on the way in, with no step to fall between. Faces that only run along the way draw nothing. What the latch's box holds is the latch's: each face is cut at the box, as a tab's face can run on into its part, and where the tab is on the socket's part, that part is cut there too. Anything else in the way, and the joint **fails**: it can't be put together that way, whatever the load, and nothing is solved. The same sweep along each of the six ways says which ways the parts come apart past nothing but the latch (`comes_out`), so which way the tail does go in (`goes_in`), or that none does.

The latch is credited with holding only where `bend` reads `OK`. Where `bend` reads it over the material's limit, or can't read it pushed that way at all, as a stub whose push runs along it from its root, the joint **fails**, with `bend`'s words, and nothing is solved: a latch that can't get on holds nothing, lifted or pulled. A tab that passes nothing on the way in has nothing to bend.

The tab's strain has `bend`'s own second method, its beam, said in the reply.

The solve ties its faces all over, as every joint's, and tied faces pull as well as they push; a real flank only pushes. So what the solve gives is what the joint carries, and that's put back on its faces as pushes alone.

That's a footing's bearing pressure with no tension, on faces that lie every way. The shift and the turn that balance what the joint carries are found by Newton's method on a sum that's convex, so there's one answer. Each face has its `push_n`, how much of it bears (`bears_mm2`) and its most pressure (`pressure_mpa`), held to what the weaker of the two parts takes pressed that way to its layers.

### `fit-bed`

(`bench/fit_bed.py`.) `--bed X,Y,Z` is the bed's width, depth and height, 256 mm each unless given.

- **The sides.** A part lies on a flat face that the rest of it stands wholly above. Given STEP files, every such face is a side, and the best is one that fits, with the most flat on the bed, which is what holds a print down; the reply names the next two as well, and the record has them all (`parts.NAME.sides`). Given an assembly file, each part's `layers` says the way up it's printed, so only the two faces square to that are read, on the part's own file, before it's turned into its place. A part with no flat face there fails, and says so.
- **Whether it fits.** Laid on a side, the part is turned about the bed's upright axis by the least turn at which its footprint fits (`turned_deg`): 0 where it fits as it's drawn. A part longer than the bed is wide can still fit toward its corners. Where no turn fits, it's the turn that needs least of the bed, and the reply says how far over it is and to split it. Its height is its extent above the side.
- **It's the whole bed,** with no margin for a brim or a skirt, which the slicer adds, and it doesn't read whether the side needs supports.
- **Verdict:** `OK` when every part has a side that fits, `FAIL` naming each that doesn't. `parts.NAME.fits` is each part's.
- **Second method:** the exact solid turned the same way and measured by OpenCascade's own box round it (`BRepBndLib.AddOptimal`, with no triangulation and no margin: CadQuery's own box is padded, and read a disc exactly as wide as the bed as 0.001 mm over). The two differ by the mesh's chords on a curved part. Where they land on opposite sides of the bed's size it's `DISAGREE`.
- **A nut's pocket** (mqucifer/print-shop#168 and mqucifer/print-shop#183). Where the assembly's `through` joint states a `pocket`, its nut sits in one at the far end of the second part's hole. `fit-bed` reads it from that part's own faces, where the assembly puts it (`bench/through.py`): the flat walls that stand on the hole's end, face its axis, and face each other across it in pairs. Each pair holds the nut by two flats: six walls are a hex pocket, and two with an open end a slot the nut slides into. `pockets.NAME.N` has each bolt's: `across_flats_mm`, the narrowest pair; `play_mm`, half of that less the nut's size across its flats; `depth_mm`; `goes_in`, `turn_deg` and `off_axis_mm`; and `fits`.
  - **The nut going in** (mqucifer/print-shop#183). Walls that hold the flats in pairs don't say whether the corners get between them: on eval-table-1 the frame's pockets were 8.2 mm squares round M5 nuts, which hold the 8 mm flats with 0.1 mm of play, and they passed, though in any turn an M5 nut is at least 8.79 x cos 15° = 8.49 mm wide along one side or the other. So the nut is read as a hexagon at its least size across its corners, the table's `e`, and turned round its middle, a quarter of a degree at a time and then a thousandth near the best, until it's clear of every wall's plane. Its middle is on the bolt's axis, or as far off it as the bolt can move in the narrower of the two parts' holes, the hole's radius less half the bolt's width, and no further (`room_in_hole_mm`): the nut is on the bolt. Where some turn and place clear every wall, `goes_in` is true, with the turn and how far off the axis it is. Where none does, the reply gives the turn at which, with its middle on the axis, its corners pass the walls least, each wall they pass, toward which way from the axis and by how much, and how far off the axis it would have to be, where it could go in at all.
  - **It fails** where the pocket is narrower than the nut's flats, which then don't go in; where the nut's corners don't go in, as above; where it's as wide as the nut's least size across its corners, so the nut turns in it; where its play isn't what the file states, within 0.005 mm; and where no two flat walls face each other there. A pocket shallower than the nut is said, and isn't a failure: the nut stands proud.
  - **Second method:** the part's fine mesh cut square to the bolt half way up the pocket, and the nearest that outline comes to the axis, against the nearest wall by its faces; and with the nut's hexagon where the walls' reading puts it, how far that outline reaches into it (`into_nut_by_mesh_mm`), against how far the outline of the walls' planes does (`into_nut_mm`): a wall's deepest point inside the hexagon, from its nearest side. Past 0.01 mm apart, either, it's `DISAGREE`. A curved wall, such as a slot's round end, is in the mesh's outline and not the walls', and so reads `DISAGREE` where it's in the nut's way.
  - **What it leaves out:** the nut's own corners are at their least, so a nut whose corners are sharper than the standard's least is bigger than this reads; and the walls are read as planes, so a pocket whose walls don't make a convex outline is read as smaller than it is.
  - **Planted** (`bed/pocket*`): an M5 nut, 8 mm across its flats and at least 8.79 across its corners, through a 5.5 mm hole, so the bolt has 0.25 mm to move. A hex pocket 8.4 mm across, 4 mm deep, stated at 0.2: `OK`, 0.200 mm a side by its faces and 4.2000 mm to its nearest wall by both methods, the nut 0.70 mm proud, going in square on with 0.3938 mm to spare by both. The same pair turned on its side reads the same. A slot 8.4 mm wide, open at the plate's edge: `OK`, three walls in one pair; the nut goes in turned 17.13° with its middle 0.085 mm toward the open end, its corners passing all three walls by 0.045 mm with it on the axis. 7.9 mm across: `FAIL`, the nut doesn't go in. 9.0: `FAIL`, the nut turns in it. A round pocket: `FAIL`, no flat walls. Stated at 0.3 where it's drawn at 0.2: `FAIL`, saying both. **A square 8.2 mm across, stated at 0.1:** `FAIL`, turned its best, 15°, its corners pass each of the four walls by 0.145 mm, and the walls reach 0.1302 mm into it by both methods. An octagon 8.75 mm across, stated at 0.375: `OK`, turned 7.5° off a flat, 0.0154 mm clear. eval-table-1's own files, read once in a scratch folder and not kept: its frame's four squares fail the same way, and its leg's eight hex pockets go in turned 30°.
- **Planted** (`tests/bench_cases/make_fixtures.py`, `bed/`): a 120 mm beam fits as it's drawn, on its largest flat; a 300 x 30 one fits a 256 mm bed only turned 37.75°, at 255.57 x 207.39; a 400 mm one needs 304.1 mm at its best, 48.1 over, and fails, saying to split it; a rail 300 mm long printed standing is 44 mm too tall, and lying down fits; a disc 256 mm across fits, and one 256.2 doesn't. At the table's planned size, a leg 520 mm tall fits no way round, standing or lying.

## Limits

- **Docker's disk is shared,** with LiteLLM's database, crew's workspaces and any running tick, and it filled on 2026-11-01. There are two images. The second, `print-shop/cae:0.1` (mqucifer/print-shop#142), which runs `load` too with nothing added to it (mqucifer/print-shop#156), is 3.19 GB on the Mac, of at most 4 GB: micromamba 2.0.5 by its digest, and conda-forge's FreeCAD 1.1.3, CalculiX 2.23, gmsh 4.15.2 and what they need, 296 packages on arm64 and 306 on amd64, each by its version, build and MD5 (`bench/cae-image/lock-*.txt`), so a build solves nothing. `build cae` refuses if it, and the first image where that isn't built yet, could leave under 5 GB free, and `build` counts the second the same way; `check` holds each to its limit and names any other tag of the second left on the machine. The first, `print-shop/bench:0.15`, is 2.13 GB, of at most 4 GB. It holds what 0.2 did, with the bench's version as a label: the bench code is mounted, so 0.3 marks the bench that reads islands and holes (mqucifer/print-shop#27), 0.4 the one with `light` and `thickness` (mqucifer/print-shop#37), 0.5 the `capture` that says what the frame lacks over or under a pane (mqucifer/print-shop#46), 0.6 the `info` that reads small flat faces, the `capture` that names other plays and the sheet that marks files by name (mqucifer/print-shop#54), 0.7 the enclosure's `edges` and `clearance --max-gap` (mqucifer/print-shop#60), the needs that follow the part's files and the check for curves stored as strips (mqucifer/print-shop#59), and `layout` and `capture`'s rim figures (mqucifer/print-shop#61), 0.8 `clearance --lift` and its verdict on OCCT's exact gap, `edges` by group and by place, and `capture`'s and `rays`' clearer replies (mqucifer/print-shop#65), and the refinement pass's turn onto the side the run names and its reading of gap fill inside a wall (mqucifer/print-shop#66), 0.9 `clearance`'s gaps by place and one rule for a figure at its limit (mqucifer/print-shop#72), 0.10 `clearance --slide` and `HELD` (mqucifer/print-shop#76), 0.11 the replies that say where, `clearance`'s bodies and overlaps, `light`'s, `thin`'s and `capture`'s, and `faces` and `slice` (mqucifer/print-shop#77), 0.12 the slide that reads a contact where the parts already touch and keeps a verdict both methods agree on (mqucifer/print-shop#84), and the `info` that counts runs of strips, not narrow flat faces (mqucifer/print-shop#85), 0.13 networkx, which trimesh needs to split a mesh whose body has an open seam, with the slide's `HELD BY A SEPARATE BODY` (mqucifer/print-shop#122), 0.14 the slide's tie round the part (mqucifer/print-shop#135) and `wall --z` (mqucifer/print-shop#136), and 0.15 the slide that follows a widening contact back by its curve (mqucifer/print-shop#143) and the `info` that stacks an STL's sections between its level faces (mqucifer/print-shop#144). Each up to 0.12 shares all of 0.2's layers; 0.13 shares them up to the bench's own install, whose new pin builds that layer and the ones after it again, 2.13 GB in all, and 0.14 and 0.15 share all of 0.13's (0.1 had no `lookup` or `docs` indexes; any of them may still be on a machine). `build` refuses if that could leave under 5 GB free, and `check` says if the image or the disk is over. Its first layers are mqucifer/model-performance's CadQuery image, so where that's present it costs tens of MB.
- **A container**, of either image, has no network, 2 CPUs, 3 GiB with no swap, a read-only image, 1 GB of `/tmp`, the invoking user, no capabilities, no log on Docker's disk, a 2 GiB limit on any file, and 600 s. `/work` is the only thing it writes, and the bench's code is mounted read-only, so changing the code never costs another image. Every package is pinned.
- **It moves to another machine** by clone and `build`: CI builds it for amd64 and runs the planted cases there, and the Mac builds it for arm64.
- **Accuracy:** the mesh is at 0.002 mm, and a figure is good to the band it prints. Nothing here has been compared with a print. A pause layer assumes a layer takes its outline from its mid-height. Orca has since agreed with it on our fixtures and on three approved runs (the refinement pass, mqucifer/print-shop#51), except at a tie: a ceiling on a layer's mid-height, where `pause` closes the pocket a layer early and Orca leaves it open. The Centauri's mid-print pause hasn't been run.
- **`bench/refine.py`** isn't a command: it's the refinement pass's geometry, started by the harness. It meshes STEP for Orca at Orca's own GUI defaults and holds the mesh to OCCT, and reads Orca's lines against the part.

## Tests

Planted fixtures are made from scratch by `tests/bench_cases/make_fixtures.py`, with right answers from arithmetic: `uv run pytest -q -m bench`. `tests/test_pieces.py` draws its own pictures and maps (`bench_cases/planted_pieces.py`): a clean panel, one with one of each rule break, a curved scene and two maps as rules would write them. `tests/test_pieces_local.py` uses three more of them, a panel with a dark patch, one with half a line faint, and a band of lead between three pieces, for the fixes that work on one place. `tests/test_pieces_dome.py` writes its own bands files for a dome of the planned size, and bends each slice back by its file's angles in plain Python, against the ellipse. `tests/test_pieces_seams.py` builds two neighbours with seams, their came strips and rings, and places them, with each angle worked out from the rows' corners by cross products; its controls are two slices built square-edged, a neighbour built for another gap, a lip that leaves a groove too near an opening, today's H strip over the outer face, a bar over an opening, a strip made for another gap, and rings made for a thinner frame. The second image's are made in FreeCAD by `tests/cae_cases/make_fixtures.py`: `uv run pytest -q -m cae`, which needs both images, as one test follows the slide's reply into `fit-on` and `bend`. `load`'s cases are there too, and `fit-bed`'s in the first image's. `tests/test_load.py` needs no image: the assembly file, the bought parts' tables, a through-bolt's hole and its nut's pocket read from plain rows of faces, a dovetail's joint in the file and its faces' pushes against a wedge worked by hand (mqucifer/print-shop#170), the arithmetic that shares a joint's force between its bolts, statics across a joint, an element's forces, and the furniture toolset. No Sponsor's design, or anything derived from one, is in this repository, and `scripts/bench_local_run.py` refuses to write inside it. `tests/test_bench_local.py` (`-m bench_local`) runs on the Sponsor's exported frame where it's present, and skips elsewhere. The frame's heights and play are in `~/print-shop/bench-local.json` on the Mac, off this repository. `scripts/bench_local_run.py triforce --out DIR` saves that run's figures to a folder off the repository, and `reference --out DIR` runs the commands over our reference parts in mqucifer/model-performance.
